So I'm reading this book, "The Kill Chain". I think it's written by a very noob 8th grader; it's the same thing essentially. The whole content of the book can be delivered in one blog post; there was no need to write a fucking book about it.

Christian Brose is this guy who is now the strategy officer of Andret, a company which is trying to tell the US government, "Hey look, the whole world is trying to dominate the United States and therefore it needs to up its military game and for that you need to give us money." It's a classic capitalist move where they are trying to create demand through fear and hubris. That's point number 1.

If you look at the content of the book itself, it's just "The United States is falling behind, give us money, we will make it better." The current Pentagon, Congress, and Defense Industrial Complex is not sufficient to counter the resurgence of China as a potential candidate who wants to get hold of world power. Then there is Russia, of course, and then there is the Middle East and then there is Japan and then blah blah blah.

I mean the whole book is material-less; it's disappointing. There are a lot of claims which are not backed by evidence. There are no quotations. I would have expected somebody who has been a senior security advisor to the United States government to have more insights and more things to cover. Fucking Noam Chomsky did a better job when he wrote "How The World Works", man. He actually put things in perspective by showing us how things were rather than telling us how things are without substantiating them with evidence. Yeah I'm pretty disappointed with this book; don't read it. 

The high moral ground of the US military as opposed to pointing fingers at the Chinese Communist Party and Soviet Union/Russia? Presenting half one-sided facts and using it to justify the single-minded immoral development of lethal autonomous weapons. Ironically pointing out to reports and polls and acknowledging that they should not be interpreted and then still going ahead to interpret it and use it to justify development of lethal autonomous weapons. 

Asking questions about the ethical model and practical implications of lethal autonomous weapons but not concluding anything by giving any answers, just asking questions and leaving the conversation incomplete and still jumping to a conclusion of developing autonomous weapons by pointing fingers at others and saying that, "Oh they are developing autonomous weapons." 

Honestly, lost interest midway, it was that bad. 

Last two chapters discussing the American Congressional-DoD-Private sector trinity is worth some value. 

The fundamental issue is that autonomous systems are not predictable. I have authority to speak because I literally engineer these systems. And to hand them a gun and let it "decide" who to kill and who not to kill is a war crime. 

The author obviously has a vested interest. He is a capitalist and gains a lot by peddling his warped theory. His company literally makes autonomous killer machines as he calls it.
